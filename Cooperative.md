# Scribb Workspace Type: Community Cooperative

We are adding a new Scribb workspace type called **Community Cooperative**.

A Community Cooperative is an ongoing, member-governed organization where people pool money, skills, materials, connections, or other resources into a shared treasury or resource pool. Members hold participation positions in the cooperative, vote on how resources are used, select projects, approve budgets and timelines, and share in any financial or nonfinancial benefits created by the group.

This workspace is not limited to one specific project. The cooperative may continuously propose, fund, manage, and complete multiple projects over time.

## Example Use Case

A group of Liberians in the diaspora creates a cooperative to support development in Liberia.

Members contribute money and receive participation units representing their position in the cooperative. Members can propose projects such as:

* Building community wells
* Supporting schools
* Funding agricultural projects
* Investing in community businesses
* Purchasing shared equipment
* Supporting infrastructure projects

Members vote on which projects to pursue, how much money to allocate, who will manage the project, the expected timeline, and how results or benefits will be distributed.

Depending on the cooperative’s rules, members may also be allowed to transfer or resell their participation positions to another eligible person.

## Core Positioning

**Community Cooperative**

*Pool resources, hold a position, govern together, and share the benefits.*

The Community Cooperative workspace should focus on five main areas:

1. Membership and participation positions
2. Shared resources and treasury management
3. Democratic governance
4. Project implementation
5. Transparent benefits and accountability

# Unique Features of the Community Cooperative Workspace

## 1. Participation Positions

Each member may hold a defined position in the cooperative.

A position may be represented through:

* Participation units
* Membership units
* Contribution units
* Cooperative shares
* Percentage interests
* Equal membership positions

The workspace must allow the cooperative to define what a position represents.

A position may provide:

* Voting rights
* Proposal rights
* Access to cooperative services
* Eligibility for financial distributions
* Eligibility for nonfinancial benefits
* Priority access to cooperative opportunities
* Recognition of a member’s contribution

Participation positions should not automatically be described as securities or investments. The cooperative administrator must define the legal and economic nature of the positions.

## 2. Configurable Ownership Structure

The cooperative should be able to select its participation model.

Supported models should include:

### Equal Membership

Every approved member holds one equal position.

### Unit-Based Participation

Members may hold different numbers of participation units based on their contributions.

### Contribution-Based Participation

A member’s position is calculated from money, labor, materials, expertise, or other approved contributions.

### Hybrid Participation

Some rights are equal among all members, while other benefits are based on participation units or contributions.

The cooperative should be able to define:

* Total authorized units
* Units currently issued
* Unit price or contribution requirement
* Minimum and maximum units per member
* Whether additional units can be issued
* Whether positions can increase or decrease
* Whether positions expire
* Whether positions may be inherited
* Whether positions may be transferred or sold

## 3. Position Transfers and Marketplace

The workspace should support controlled transfers of participation positions.

A member may request to:

* Transfer a position to another member
* Sell units to another eligible participant
* Return units to the cooperative
* Donate units
* Transfer units to an heir or beneficiary

The cooperative should be able to configure:

* Whether transfers are allowed
* Who is eligible to receive a position
* Whether board approval is required
* Whether member approval is required
* Whether the cooperative has the first right to purchase
* Transfer fees
* Minimum holding periods
* Maximum position limits
* Transfer restrictions
* Required identity verification
* Required supporting documents

Every transfer should create a transparent record containing:

* Current holder
* New holder
* Units transferred
* Transfer type
* Transfer value, when applicable
* Date
* Required approvals
* Supporting documents
* Final status

An optional internal bulletin board or marketplace may allow members to list positions they wish to transfer. Transactions should only be completed after all cooperative rules and approvals have been satisfied.

## 4. Shared Treasury

The cooperative should have a shared treasury dashboard.

The treasury should track:

* Member contributions
* Membership or participation fees
* Donations
* Grants
* Project income
* Investment income
* Asset-sale income
* Operating expenses
* Project allocations
* Distributions to members
* Reserve funds
* Restricted funds
* Available balance
* Committed balance

Every transaction should show:

* Amount
* Source or recipient
* Purpose
* Category
* Date
* Supporting evidence
* Approvals
* Related project or proposal
* Recorded-by information

The system should allow administrators to create multiple funds, such as:

* General Fund
* Project Fund
* Emergency Fund
* Community Development Fund
* Administrative Fund
* Reserve Fund
* Benefit Distribution Fund

## 5. Contributions Beyond Money

Members should be able to contribute more than cash.

Contribution types may include:

* Money
* Labor
* Professional services
* Materials
* Equipment
* Land
* Transportation
* Technical expertise
* Business connections
* Volunteer hours
* Intellectual property

The cooperative should determine whether noncash contributions:

* Earn participation units
* Count toward membership requirements
* Provide voting rights
* Provide benefit eligibility
* Are treated only as voluntary support

Each contribution should include a description, agreed value when applicable, supporting evidence, approval status, and the participation units awarded.

## 6. Cooperative Governance

The Community Cooperative workspace must include configurable governance rules.

Voting models should include:

### One Member, One Vote

Every active member has equal voting power.

### Unit-Weighted Voting

Voting power is based on the number of participation units held.

### Contribution-Weighted Voting

Voting power is based on qualifying contributions.

### Hybrid Voting

Some decisions use equal voting while others use unit-weighted voting.

### Delegated Voting

Members may delegate their voting authority to another eligible member for a specific proposal or defined period.

The cooperative should configure:

* Voting eligibility
* Quorum
* Approval threshold
* Voting period
* Secret or public ballots
* Whether abstentions count
* Whether votes may be changed before closing
* Whether certain decisions require a supermajority
* Whether different proposal types use different voting rules

## 7. Proposal Management

Eligible members should be able to submit proposals.

Proposal categories may include:

* New project
* Project funding
* Budget change
* Leadership appointment
* Policy change
* New member approval
* Position issuance
* Position transfer
* Benefit distribution
* Asset purchase
* Asset sale
* Partnership
* Contract approval
* Emergency action
* Cooperative dissolution

Each proposal should contain:

* Title
* Summary
* Problem being addressed
* Recommended action
* Requested budget
* Funding source
* Expected benefits
* Risks
* Responsible persons
* Timeline
* Milestones
* Supporting documents
* Discussion period
* Voting period
* Required approval threshold

Proposal statuses should include:

* Draft
* Submitted
* Under review
* Open for discussion
* Open for voting
* Approved
* Rejected
* Withdrawn
* In implementation
* Completed
* Suspended

## 8. Project Portfolio

Approved proposals may become projects.

The cooperative should be able to manage multiple projects from one workspace.

Each project should include:

* Project title
* Location
* Description
* Project manager
* Oversight committee
* Approved budget
* Funds released
* Funds spent
* Start date
* Target completion date
* Current status
* Milestones
* Contractors or partners
* Beneficiaries
* Risks and issues
* Updates
* Photos, videos, receipts, and documents
* Completion report
* Impact report

Members should be able to see the complete relationship between:

* The original proposal
* The vote
* The approved budget
* Treasury transactions
* Project milestones
* Supporting evidence
* Final results

## 9. Benefits Management

The cooperative should support both financial and nonfinancial benefits.

Financial benefits may include:

* Surplus distributions
* Patronage payments
* Revenue sharing
* Refunds
* Project-generated income
* Cooperative dividends, where legally permitted

Nonfinancial benefits may include:

* Access to cooperative services
* Discounts
* Shared equipment access
* Training
* Business opportunities
* Priority participation
* Community recognition
* Access to land, housing, facilities, or other resources
* Voting and governance rights

The cooperative should define:

* Who qualifies for a benefit
* How benefits are calculated
* Whether benefits are equal or position-based
* Whether benefits are based on participation or contribution
* When benefits are issued
* Whether benefits may be transferred
* Whether inactive members qualify
* Required approvals

Every distribution should be connected to an approved resolution or proposal.

## 10. Member Entry and Exit

The workspace should support a full member lifecycle.

### Joining

A person may:

* Apply to join
* Receive an invitation
* Purchase or receive participation units
* Submit identity information
* Accept the cooperative’s rules
* Make the required contribution
* Receive approval

### Active Membership

An active member may:

* Hold a position
* Vote
* Submit proposals
* Participate in discussions
* Receive cooperative benefits
* View authorized records
* Join committees

### Leaving

A member may:

* Transfer their position
* Sell eligible units
* Return units to the cooperative
* Resign
* Be removed under the cooperative’s rules
* Designate a beneficiary or successor

The system should preserve the complete historical record of past members and previous position holders.

## 11. Committees and Leadership

The cooperative may create leadership and oversight roles such as:

* Chairperson
* Secretary
* Treasurer
* Project Manager
* Board Member
* Trustee
* Auditor
* Finance Committee
* Project Committee
* Membership Committee
* Procurement Committee
* Dispute Committee

Each role should have configurable permissions, appointment dates, term limits, responsibilities, and removal procedures.

## 12. Transparency and Audit Trail

Transparency is a core purpose of the workspace.

Scribb should preserve an immutable or tamper-evident history of:

* Contributions
* Position issuance
* Position transfers
* Proposals
* Discussions
* Votes
* Decisions
* Treasury transactions
* Project expenditures
* Documents
* Leadership appointments
* Benefit distributions
* Policy changes

Every important action should show:

* Who performed it
* What changed
* When it happened
* Why it happened
* Which approval authorized it
* Which supporting evidence was provided

Sensitive personal or financial information may remain restricted while summaries and accountability records are made visible to authorized members or the public.

# Important Product Distinctions

The Community Cooperative differs from a Membership Group because members do more than pay dues and receive membership benefits. They jointly govern shared resources, hold participation positions, approve projects, and may share in the value or benefits created by the cooperative.

It differs from a Community Project because it is permanent or ongoing and may manage many projects rather than being created for one specific objective.

It differs from a traditional investment workspace because participation may produce financial or nonfinancial benefits, and members actively govern the cooperative rather than merely investing passively.

It differs from a donation or crowdfunding platform because contributors may maintain an ongoing position, exercise voting rights, participate in decisions, transfer their positions, and qualify for future benefits.

# Legal and Compliance Considerations

The workspace should be flexible because cooperatives operate under different laws and organizational structures.

Scribb should not automatically promise:

* Investment returns
* Profit
* Dividends
* Guaranteed resale value
* Liquidity
* Legal ownership
* Securities compliance

When a cooperative allows transferable positions or financial returns, the setup process should display a notice recommending that the organization obtain appropriate legal, tax, cooperative, and securities advice.

Scribb provides governance, transparency, recordkeeping, and administrative tools. The cooperative remains responsible for determining its legal structure and complying with applicable laws.
